Notes for App Review

Notes · App Review Information → Notes
Baby Mo is a paid Islamic learning app (one-time purchase) for children aged 2–8 with 19 games: daily and Ramadan du'as, prayer and wudhu guides, Arabic letters and 106 Arabic words, a letter-hunt island, a tennis-learning game, caring for farm animals and a little garden. There are no accounts, ads, subscriptions, in-app purchases, chat or social features. • No login is needed: open the app and choose a language. • Location is optional and only used for prayer times and the Qibla compass in the Prayer tab. If permission is denied, the user can search for a city by hand — the list of 27,000+ places (GeoNames, credited in the app) is bundled, so the search works offline. Prayer times and the Qibla are calculated on the device; no location or other data ever leaves the device. • The child's nickname, stars and progress are stored only on the device. • Arabic read-aloud plays recordings bundled with the app (the device's speech voice is a fallback). There are no analytics or third-party SDKs, and every link out of the app and every share option is behind a parental gate (a maths question for grown-ups). • All games work offline. The app's only network request is the update check on the Version row in the Parents tab: at most once a day it asks Apple's App Store lookup service (itunes.apple.com/lookup, with the app's bundle ID only — no user data) whether a newer version exists. Offline it simply does nothing, and opening the App Store from there is behind the parental gate. • The archive includes a small Apple Watch app: five daily du'as in the same recorded voice, a tasbih counter, on-device prayer times (the parent picks a city by hand — the watch never uses location), a Qibla compass for that city (compass heading only, no location permission), Ramadan countdowns with a practice-fast tracker, and a simple pet check-in. Optional reminders — wudu, suhoor/iftar and the “Wake up with Mo” morning alarm — are local notifications; the watch asks for notification permission only when a parent turns one on. Like the iPhone app it is fully offline, has no accounts and collects no data. • “Baby Mo Du'a” is also a Shortcuts action: it plays the travel du'a from the bundled recordings, and a parent can add it to a CarPlay automation. It sends nothing off the device. • During Ramadan (Umm al-Qura calendar on the device) the home screen shows a Ramadan look; it switches on and off by itself.
