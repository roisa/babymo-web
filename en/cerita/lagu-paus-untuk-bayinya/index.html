A Whale's Song for Her Baby

🐙 Sea & NatureBook 4 of 10← PreviousNext →

Thunder crashes and Mo is scared. Umi tells him about a mother whale who swims thousands of kilometres, singing very softly — just for her baby.

That night the rain poured down. BOOM! Thunder crashed. Mo hid under his blanket, trembling.

Umi came and hugged him. "Would you like to hear a story about a mother whale and her baby?" Mo gave a tiny nod.

In a cold, wide sea lived a mother whale and her baby. The baby was as long as a car, but next to his mother he looked very small.

Every year they swim a very long way — thousands of kilometres — from the cold sea to the warm sea.

One night a storm came over the sea. The waves roared. The baby whale got scared and stopped swimming.

The mother whale swam close until their bodies touched. "I'm here," she said. And she began to sing, very softly.

Did you know? Humpback whale mothers whisper to their babies very quietly, so only the baby can hear and predators don't know they're there.

"You sing softly to me too, Umi," Mo whispered. Umi smiled and began to hum.

Baby whales can't breathe underwater. When he got tired, his mother gently pushed him up to the surface so he could breathe the air.

The journey was very long. The mother whale hardly ate for months, because she kept caring for her baby and feeding him her milk.

"Months without eating?" Mo whispered. "The mother whale must love her baby so much." "That's how all mothers are," Umi answered softly.

Umi kissed Mo's forehead. "The Prophet ﷺ loved to kiss his grandchildren. He said whoever shows mercy will be shown mercy by Allah."

"Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy." (Bukhari and Muslim)

At last they reached the warm blue sea. The storm was long gone, and the baby whale swam happily beside his mother.

Outside, the rain was easing too. There was no more thunder. Mo's eyes were getting heavy.

"Umi," Mo whispered, "when you're old and you feel scared, I'll be the one who sings to you." Umi held him for a long, long time.

Which song do you love most when Umi or Abi sings it to you? 🐋

A card for parents

When Your Child Fears Thunder or the Dark

  1. 1Name the fear ("That was really loud, wasn't it?") and stay close — never "that's nothing to be scared of".
  2. 2Explain simply ("thunder is the sound of electricity in the clouds") and teach the du'a for hearing thunder.
  3. 3Keep the same bedtime ritual every night — the same song, story or du'a helps children feel safe.
