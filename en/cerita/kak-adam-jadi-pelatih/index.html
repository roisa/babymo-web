Kak Adam the Coach

🎾 Kak Adam's Tennis JourneyBook 20 of 23← PreviousNext →

Baby Mo and Baby Ais want to play tennis like Kak Adam. But the ball is too fast — and Kak Adam's patience is tested!

"Kak Adam, teach us tennis!" Baby Mo and Baby Ais stood at the door with toy rackets.

"Easy!" said Kak Adam. "Watch — eyes on the ball, turn, swing!" WHOOSH! The ball shot off like lightning.

Baby Mo tried. Swing… miss. Swing… miss again. The ball rolled under the bench.

"Not like that, Mo! I already told you!" Kak Adam's voice got loud. Baby Mo looked at his feet.

Baby Ais whispered, "Kak… did Abi get cross when the ball bonked your head?"

Kak Adam went quiet. He remembered Abi laughing, kneeling down, and saying, "Let's learn slowly."

Kak Adam knelt in front of Baby Mo. "Sorry, Mo. I went too fast. Let's start with the slowest thing."

"What's the slowest thing in the world?" Baby Ais wondered. "A snail!" "No… a BALLOON!"

Kak Adam blew up a red balloon. It floated down slooowly. Baby Mo had time to watch it… and hit it. POK!

"I can do it! I can do it!" Up went the balloon again. Baby Ais hit it to Kak Adam. Kak Adam hit it to Mo.

Up and down went the balloon a hundred times. Nobody fell. Nobody got cross. Everybody laughed.

Then Kak Adam gave them a tennis ball, bouncing it gently. "Bounce it on your racket. One… two…"

"…THREE!" Baby Ais made it three times! Baby Mo, twice! Kak Adam clapped the loudest.

Abi watched from a distance and smiled. "Masha Allah. A patient big brother makes his little ones brave."

"He is not one of us who does not show mercy to the young and respect to the old." (Tirmidhi)

"Kak Adam, practise again tomorrow?" "Definitely. Your coach never gives up!" Kak Adam hugged them both.

That night Kak Adam prayed, "O Allah, make me a patient big brother." Who has patiently taught you something? 🎈

A card for parents

When an Older Sibling Teaches

  1. 1Give the older child one small thing to teach, like putting on shoes.
  2. 2Remind them: "You were taught slowly once too."
  3. 3Praise the older child's patience in front of the younger one, not just the result.
