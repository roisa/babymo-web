> *"Eat sahur, for in sahur there is blessing." (Bukhari 1923, Muslim 1095)*

Mo ate rice, an egg and a banana. Ais drank her water right to the bottom. Gulp, gulp, gulp! "So we're strong till the drum!"

Abi explained, "You're still little, so you'll fast until the noon drum first. After lunch, carry on again if you feel strong."

"Imsak, imsak!" Everyone stopped eating. Ais made her intention in her heart: "O Allah, I want to fast today." So did Mo.

The morning felt perfectly normal. Mo built a tower of blocks. Ais drew pictures. "Fasting is easy!" said Mo proudly.

At ten o'clock, Mo's tummy went: grrrumble. At eleven: grrrumble-grrrumble! Mo stood in front of the fridge for a long, long time.

"Ais, Ais…" whispered Mo. "Just a tiiiny sip. Umi won't see."

Ais shook her head. "Umi might not see. But Allah sees, Mo. Fasting is a secret between us and Allah."

> *Allah says (hadith qudsi): "Every deed of the son of Adam is for himself, except fasting. It is for Me, and I Myself will reward it." (Bukhari 1904, Muslim 1151)*

Mo closed the fridge slowly. He clenched his fists. "Bismillah… I'm strong. I can do this!"

To help them forget their thirst, Umi read them a story by the fan. Whirr… whirr… Little by little, Ais fell asleep.

Then… DUM! DUM! DUM! The mosque drum boomed and the call to the noon prayer rang out. "It's time!" shouted Mo.

After the noon prayer they had lunch. Warm rice, fresh vegetables. "Alhamdulillah! This is the yummiest rice in the world!" said Mo.

That afternoon they tried again until sunset. Thirsty, hungry… but they cheered each other on: "Nearly there!"

That night, Umi stuck the first star on the calendar. "Shall we try again tomorrow?" asked Umi. "Ready!" said Ais.
