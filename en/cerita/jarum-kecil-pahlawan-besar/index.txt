"Vaccines mean injections?" Baby Mo dived behind the sofa. "I don't want to! Needles hurt!"

Abi knelt down. "I'll be honest: it feels like a quick ant bite. Then it's done. And I've got a secret trick."

The Posyandu was busy. Babies, big kids, and friendly volunteer mums. Baby Ais pointed at the hanging scale. "Is that a swing?"

"That's a dacin, a baby scale," said Abi. The twins took turns being weighed. "Masha Allah, you've grown heavier! Healthy!"

Then their height was measured on the colourful chart. "I've reached the yellow line!" Baby Ais said proudly.

While they waited, Abi told a story. "There are tiny germs in the world that make us ill. We can't see them."

"A vaccine is like teaching your body to recognise the germs, so it has a tiny army ready to protect you."

"Looking after our bodies is worship too," Abi added. "The Prophet ﷺ taught us to seek treatment and make an effort to stay healthy."

> *"Seek treatment, for Allah has not sent down a disease without also sending down its cure." (Abu Dawud and Tirmidhi)*

"Baby Mo!" called the midwife. Baby Mo's legs wobbled. Abi sat him on his lap and held him tight.

"Now the secret trick," Abi whispered. "Imagine a birthday candle. Breathe in… then blow sloooowly… fwoooo."

Fwoooo… prick! Like an ant bite. Baby Mo winced, a little tear came out… and then it was over!

The midwife stuck on a star plaster. "You were so brave." "But I cried," said Baby Mo. "You can be brave and cry at the same time," said Abi.

Baby Ais's turn. She blew her pretend candle even longer than Baby Mo. Fwooooooo! Then she high-fived her twin.

That night Baby Mo felt a little warm. Abi gave him a drink and a cuddle. "That's your tiny army doing its training."
