2e:T6a8,That morning Ais didn't jump out of bed like usual. Her eyes were droopy and her cheeks were red.

Umi put her hand on Ais's forehead, then fetched the thermometer. “Thirty-eight and a half. Ais has a fever.”

Baby Mo peeked round the door. “Ais, let's play ball!” But Ais just shook her head slowly.

“Ais's body is fighting germs,” Umi explained. “They're tiny, but her body is stronger. She needs to rest.”

“I want to help Ais beat the germs!” said Mo. “What can I do?”

Job one: bringing water. Mo walked ever so slowly so it wouldn't spill.

“Small sips, Ais.” Ais drank, then gave a little smile. “Thank you, Mo.”

Job two: Umi made warm soup. Mo helped stir — slowly, slowly, slowly.

Job three: making du'a. Umi taught Mo the du'a for someone ill, and Mo said it while stroking Ais's head.

> *The Prophet ﷺ said, “No Muslim is struck by tiredness, illness, worry or hurt — even a thorn that pricks them — except that Allah wipes away some of their mistakes because of it.” (Bukhari 5641, Muslim 2573)*

Job four: reading a story. Mo can't read yet, so he told the story from the pictures.

His story was very silly — the cat could fly and the elephant rode a bike. Ais laughed so much she coughed.

That night Ais slept soundly. Mo fell asleep in the chair beside her, still holding the book.
