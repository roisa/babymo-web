5b:T82b,The big day had come: the junior tournament final! The court was in a stadium, with stands full of people and a huge scoreboard.

In the changing room, Kak Adam's hands felt cold. "Abi, what if I lose in front of everyone?"

Abi held his hands. "You've trained for months — that's your effort. Now leave the result to Allah — that's trust."

"It's like the man who asked the Prophet ﷺ whether to tie up his camel or just trust in Allah. He answered: tie it, and then trust."

> *"Tie it, and then trust in Allah." (Tirmidhi 2517; graded hasan)*

They made dua together. "Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil — Allah is enough for us, and He is the best protector."

> *"Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil." — Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs. (Qur'an, Ali 'Imran 3:173)*

In the stands, Mo, Ais and Umi held up a banner: "GO, KAK ADAM!" The umpire sat in the tall chair. "Play!"

His opponent, Dio, played really fast. Kak Adam was tense and rushed. The score was 0–4! The crowd murmured.

At the changeover, Kak Adam sat with his head down. Then he remembered his calm routine: a long breath… look at the strings… just one point.

"Don't try to win the match. Just win the next point." He bounced the ball three times — bop, bop, bop — and served.

POK! His serve went in. One point. Then another. Then another. Slowly the score crept closer: 3–4… 5–5…

A long rally! Dio hit to the left, Kak Adam shuffled across — shk shk shk — and sent it back with a two-handed backhand!

Match point. Dio's ball caught the net. "Game, set and match — Adam!" Kak Adam dropped to his knees. "Alhamdulillah!"

Then he ran to the net and hugged Dio. "You played amazingly. You made me play better."

Kak Adam received the trophy and a medal. He looked for Abi in the stands and mouthed from afar, "I tied the camel, Abi!"

That night the trophy stood on the shelf. But what Kak Adam wrote in his training notebook was: "One point, one breath, one dua."
